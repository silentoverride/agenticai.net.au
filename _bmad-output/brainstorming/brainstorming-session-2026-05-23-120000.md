**Facilitator:** Pi (AI Assistant)

## Session Overview

**Topic:** Staff Portal for Agentic AI — operational hub for managing clients, report errors, client meetings, and meeting preparation.

**Goals:**
- Feature requirements / functional scope
- Screen layouts and navigation structure
- Prioritised build order (MVP vs future)
- Risk analysis and edge cases

### Session Setup

Lorin is the business owner/admin of the Agentic AI Business Assessment platform. The existing system has separate client portal, operator dashboard, gates, human-assist, and calibration pages — but the admin control surface needs to be designed as the central operational hub. Focus areas: client lifecycle management, error surfacing/remediation, meeting scheduling/preparation workflows.

## Technique Selection

**Approach:** AI-Recommended Techniques
**Analysis Context:** Staff Portal Admin Control Surface with focus on feature scope, screen design, prioritisation, and risk analysis

**Recommended Techniques:**

- **Question Storming:** Define the full scope of capabilities by generating questions first — before jumping to solutions
- **Mind Mapping:** Organise scope into coherent screen groups, navigation flows, and information architecture
- **Resource Constraints:** Force true priority decisions by imposing extreme build constraints (e.g. 4-week deadline)
- **Reverse Brainstorming:** Identify failure modes, edge cases, and gaps by asking what could go wrong

**AI Rationale:** 4-phase sequence mirroring Lorin's four goals — scope → structure → priorities → risks — each technique builds naturally on the previous.

### Mind Mapping — Navigation Refinement

**Decision from Lorin:** Keep **Meetings** and **Opportunities** as separate top-level navigation areas, but visually/conceptually group them under the broader **Client Growth Engine** mental model.

**Rationale:** Staff will enter the portal with different intents — meeting preparation, conversion opportunities, follow-ups, and offer fit. A single “Client Success” label is conceptually clean but too broad for fast navigation.

```text
Staff Portal
├── Command Center
├── Clients
├── Human Review
├── Meetings
├── Opportunities
├── Admin
└── Audit
```

**Product strategy grouping:**

```text
Client Growth Engine
├── Meetings
├── Opportunities
├── Follow-ups
├── Offer Fit
└── Client Success Notes
```

**Key refinement:** Client Profile becomes the connective tissue.

```text
Client Profile
├── Journey Status
├── Intake / Report
├── Human Review
├── Meeting Brief
├── Opportunity
├── Follow-ups
└── Activity / Audit
```

**Open structure question:** Should Command Center be split into two dashboards — “Quality Queue” and “Growth Queue” — or should it be one unified daily work queue?

**Command Center recommendation:** One unified Command Center with queue lenses/lane views rather than two separate dashboards.

```text
Command Center
├── Today’s Priority Work
├── Quality Queue
└── Growth Queue
```

**Design principle:** Command Center should be an action dashboard, not a passive reporting dashboard. Every item should answer: what is this, why does it matter, who owns it, what happens if ignored, and what is the next action?

### Mind Mapping — Follow-ups as Cross-Cutting Work Type

**Decision from Lorin:** Follow-ups should be a cross-cutting work type, not owned by Meetings or Opportunities and not top-level navigation yet.

**Rationale:** Follow-ups can originate from many sources — meeting promises, sales next actions, report review clarification, support issues, internal/admin tasks, and delayed/stuck journey states. Burying follow-ups under Meetings would hide sales actions; burying them under Opportunities would hide service/client-success actions.

```text
Follow-up source
├── Meeting promise
├── Sales/opportunity next action
├── Report review clarification
├── Client support issue
├── Admin/internal task
└── Delayed/stuck journey state
```

**Placement model:**

```text
Follow-ups
├── Surfaced in Command Center as due work
├── Shown in Client Profile as client-specific commitments
├── Linked from Meetings when created during/after a meeting
├── Linked from Opportunities when tied to sales pipeline
└── Captured in Activity/Audit when completed, missed, reassigned, or changed
```

**Key design rule:** A follow-up is not a note. A follow-up is a commitment with an owner, due date, source, status, and consequence.

**Follow-up fields/questions:**

```text
Follow-ups / Tasks
├── Cross-cutting work type
├── Appears in Command Center
├── Appears in Client Profile
├── Linked to Meetings
├── Linked to Opportunities
├── Linked to Human Review when clarification is needed
├── Owner
├── Due date
├── Source
├── Client-visible promise?
├── Status
└── Activity/audit trail
```

**Next branch:** Client Profile structure, because it is the connective tissue everything else depends on.

### Mind Mapping — Human Review Cockpit

**Core job:** Help a reviewer make a safe, auditable decision about an escalated report.

**Decision from Lorin:** Organise the cockpit around **gate findings**, with report sections as supporting navigation.

**Rationale:** The reviewer is not there to read the whole report from scratch. They are there because something was flagged. The workflow should begin with: what was flagged, why was it flagged, where is it in the report, what evidence must be inspected, and what decision is required?

```text
Human Review Cockpit
├── Gate Findings Queue
│   ├── Finding 1
│   ├── Finding 2
│   ├── Finding 3
│   └── Resolved / unresolved status
├── Finding Workspace
│   ├── Flagged report section
│   ├── Related intake evidence
│   ├── Gate explanation
│   ├── Confidence / severity
│   ├── Suggested inspection steps
│   ├── Reviewer notes
│   └── Finding-level decision
└── Whole Report Context
    ├── Full report navigation
    ├── Original / edited / regenerated versions
    ├── Overall approval checklist
    └── Final report-level decision
```

**Important distinction:** Findings are reviewed individually. The report is approved as a whole.

**Review flow:**

```text
1. Open escalated report
2. See all gate findings
3. Resolve each finding
4. Complete overall checklist
5. Make final report decision
6. Audit decision + notify next owner
```

**Gate finding lifecycle:**

```text
Gate Finding
├── Open
├── In review
├── Resolved — no change needed
├── Resolved — edited report
├── Resolved — regenerated section/report
├── Resolved — client clarification requested
├── Escalated further
└── Dismissed with override reason
```

**Product principle:** A reviewer should never have to hunt through the report to discover why they are reviewing it.

**Next branch:** Meeting Brief, because it is the growth-side equivalent of the Human Review Cockpit.

### Mind Mapping — Opportunity / Offer Fit Engine

**Core job:** Help staff identify the right commercial next step for the client, with evidence, timing, and trust guardrails.

**Decision from Lorin:** Offer fit should be **rule-grounded, AI-assisted, staff-approved**.

**Rationale:** Pure rules miss client nuance. Pure AI creates inconsistent and risky commercial recommendations. Commercial guidance needs defined guardrails, evidence-linked interpretation, human approval, and auditability.

```text
Offer Fit Engine
├── Admin-defined offer catalogue
├── Admin-defined fit rules / guardrails
├── AI-assisted interpretation of client evidence
├── Evidence-linked recommendation
├── Staff approval / adjustment
└── Audit trail for commercial decisions
```

```text
Rules decide
```

```text
AI assists with
```

**Staff approves:**

```text
├── Next action
```

**Offer Fit lifecycle:**

```text
Offer Fit Status
```

### Mind Mapping — Admin / Governance

**Core job:** Let authorised staff safely configure the operational rules that drive quality review, client growth, templates, roles, and integrations.

**Decision from Lorin:** Admin should be an operations control surface, not a passive settings area.

**Rationale:** A settings area implies “configure once, forget it,” but portal rules directly affect report safety, client trust, staff workload, sales behaviour, auditability, and operational risk. Admin should behave like a control room for operational policy.

```text
Admin / Governance
├── Rule configuration
├── Rule monitoring
├── Rule preview / test mode
├── Change approval workflow
├── Version history
├── Rollback
├── Impact analysis
└── Audit log
```

**Every admin rule change should answer:**

```text
What is changing?
Why is it changing?
Who approved it?
What clients/reports/workflows could be affected?
Can we preview the impact before publishing?
Can we roll it back?
```

**Admin structure:**

```text
Admin
├── Operational Rules
│   ├── Gate Rules
│   ├── Review Workflows
│   ├── Offer Fit Rules
│   ├── Follow-up Policies
│   └── Communication Templates
├── Business Configuration
│   ├── Offer Catalogue
│   ├── Meeting Brief Templates
│   ├── Roles & Permissions
│   └── Integrations

    ├── Pending changes
    ├── Approvals
    ├── Version history
    ├── Audit / retention
    ├── Rule impact preview
    └── Rollback
```

**Product principle:** Admin is not where rules are stored. Admin is where operational policy is safely changed.

**Preview Mode:**

```text
Preview Mode
```

### Mind Mapping — Activity, Audit & Notifications

**Core job:** Create operational memory, accountability, and timely attention across the portal.

**Product principle:** Activity records memory. Audit records accountability. Notifications route attention.

**Decision from Lorin:** Use contextual alerts first, plus a lightweight unified notification center.

**Rationale:** Staff should usually act from the surface they are already in — Command Center, Client Profile, Human Review, Meetings, Opportunities, or Admin. A heavy inbox would compete with Command Center.

```text
Notifications
├── Contextual alerts
│   ├── Command Center priority items
│   ├── Client Profile banners / chips
│   ├── Human Review queue warnings
│   ├── Meeting prep warnings
│   ├── Opportunity next-action warnings
│   └── Admin approval warnings
└── Notification Center
    ├── My notifications
    ├── Mentions / assignments
    ├── Overdue items
    ├── System/integration issues
    └── Dismissed / resolved history
```

**Key distinction:** Alerts should drive action. Notification Center should provide catch-up.

```text
Command Center = work queue
Notification Center = attention history / personal catch-up
Audit = formal accountability record
Activity Timeline = client/module memory
```

**Notification lifecycle:**

```text
Notification
├── Created
├── Seen
├── Acted on
├── Snoozed with reason
├── Reassigned
├── Resolved
├── Dismissed
└── Escalated
```

**Noise controls:**

```text
Noise controls
├── Deduplicate repeated system events
├── Group related alerts by client
├── Escalate only when action is overdue or risk increases
├── Suppress info-only events from urgent surfaces
├── Separate “FYI” from “action required”
└── Always link to the exact work item
```

**Product principle:** Do not make staff manage notifications. Use notifications to move staff to the right work.

### Resource Constraints — 4-Week MVP Cut

**Decision from Lorin:** For a 4-week first useful version, Human Review reliability matters more than Meeting/Opportunity growth flow.

**Rationale:** If staff cannot trust, approve, correct, or block reports safely, the growth flow is built on unstable ground.

**Priority order:**

```text
1. Report can be found
2. Problem can be seen
3. Human can decide
4. Decision is recorded
5. Client can receive a safe report
6. Staff can prepare for the meeting
7. Staff can identify next commercial step
```

### Reverse Brainstorming — MVP Failure Modes

**Primary concern from Lorin:** Scope creep killing the MVP, because it quietly causes the other failures.

**Core risk:** Building the appearance of operational control without the underlying lifecycle/state model.

**Risk ranking:**

```text
1. Scope creep killing the MVP
2. Unsafe report approval
3. Follow-ups being missed
4. Stale meeting prep
```

**Why unsafe approval is second:** It is the highest trust risk, but it can be controlled if the MVP stays narrow and enforces the review lifecycle properly.

**MVP non-negotiable state models:**

```text
Report
├── Generated
├── Escalated
├── In review
├── Approved
├── Rejected
├── Regeneration required
└── Clarification required

Gate Finding
├── Open
├── In review
├── Resolved
├── Overridden with reason
└── Escalated further

Follow-up
├── Open
├── Due
├── Overdue
├── Completed
├── Deferred with reason
└── Reassigned

Meeting Brief
├── Draft generated
├── Needs staff review
├── Ready for meeting
├── Stale / refresh needed
└── Completed
```

**Additional failure modes:**

31. Staff see polished screens and assume the system is safer than it really is.  
    **Fix implication:** MVP must make incomplete states obvious, not hide them behind nice UI.

32. “Approved” means different things in different places.  
    **Fix implication:** Define approval semantics once and reuse everywhere.

33. Staff can bypass the intended workflow because the system does not enforce state transitions.  
    **Fix implication:** Enforce allowed transitions, not just display statuses.

34. Follow-ups are created but not tied to a source.  
    **Fix implication:** Every follow-up must link back to meeting, review, opportunity, or client event.

35. Meeting prep looks ready even though the report is not approved.  
    **Fix implication:** Meeting Brief cannot be “Ready” while report review is unresolved.

## Idea Organization and Prioritization

### Thematic Organization

#### Theme 1: State-Model-First Operational Control

**Focus:** The Staff Portal must enforce operational lifecycles, not merely display attractive dashboards.

**Core ideas:**
- MVP should protect the Report, Gate Finding, Follow-up, and Meeting Brief state models.
- Every screen should make incomplete, blocked, stale, or unsafe states obvious.
- Workflow actions must move objects through valid transitions.
- Approval semantics must be defined once and reused everywhere.

**Pattern insight:** The foundation of trust is not navigation or UI polish; it is valid state transitions plus auditability.

#### Theme 2: Report Quality Engine

**Focus:** Human Review is the highest-priority MVP capability because unsafe report delivery undermines the whole product.

**Core ideas:**
- Human Review Queue for escalated reports.
- Gate Findings workspace organised around what was flagged, why, where, evidence, and required decision.
- Finding-level resolution before report-level approval.
- Decisions: approve, reject, regenerate, request clarification, override with reason, escalate further.
- Reason codes, reviewer notes, and audit trail are non-negotiable.

**Pattern insight:** Reviewers should never hunt through a report to discover why they are reviewing it.

#### Theme 3: Client Profile as Connective Tissue

**Focus:** The Client Profile is the single client-specific operating surface.

**Core ideas:**
- Overview should be action-oriented, not neutral.
- It should answer: what matters now, what needs action, and what should not be missed.
- Tabs/areas: Overview, Intake, Report, Review, Meetings, Opportunities, Follow-ups, Communications, Activity.
- Overview layout: Client Snapshot, What Matters Now, Quality Card, Growth Card, Follow-up Card, Recent Activity.

**Pattern insight:** The Overview should orient staff toward the next responsible action.

#### Theme 4: Client Growth Engine

**Focus:** Meetings, Offer Fit, Opportunities, and Follow-ups support growth, but should not outrank report safety in the 4-week MVP.

**Core ideas:**
- Meeting Brief should be AI-generated and staff-approved.
- Meeting prep must be blocked/stale if report review is unresolved or source data changed.
- Offer Fit should be rule-grounded, AI-assisted, and staff-approved.
- Offer Fit is not the same as Opportunity.
- Follow-ups are commitments, not notes.

**Pattern insight:** First make report delivery safe. Then make meetings useful. Then make upsell systematic.

#### Theme 5: Governance and Audit Trust Layer

**Focus:** Admin, Audit, Activity, and Notifications create safety boundaries and accountability.

**Core ideas:**
- Admin is an operations control surface, not passive settings.
- Admin rule changes should be versioned, previewable, permission-gated, and auditable.
- Activity records memory; Audit records accountability; Notifications route attention.
- Contextual alerts should drive action; Notification Center should provide catch-up.

**Pattern insight:** Operational policy must be safely changed, not merely stored.

### Prioritization Results

#### Top Priority Ideas

1. **State-model-first MVP** — prevents the portal from looking safe while remaining operationally ambiguous.
2. **Human Review Queue + Gate Findings Cockpit** — protects report delivery and client trust.
3. **Basic audit trail + reason codes** — ensures decisions are accountable.
4. **Basic follow-ups with owner, due date, source, and status** — prevents commitments from disappearing.
5. **Simple Meeting Brief** — keeps meetings useful without overbuilding growth automation.

#### Quick Win Opportunities

- Add Staff Portal navigation for Command Center, Clients, Human Review, Meetings, Opportunities, Admin, Audit.
- Build a Client Profile Overview that shows status, next action, owner, and blockers.
- Create a Human Review Queue from existing escalated/error/gate data.
- Add explicit lifecycle badges for Report, Gate Finding, Follow-up, and Meeting Brief.
- Add basic follow-up creation from review and meeting contexts.

#### Breakthrough Concepts

- **Findings are reviewed individually; the report is approved as a whole.**
- **A follow-up is not a note; it is a commitment with owner, due date, source, status, and consequence.**
- **Rules create commercial guardrails; AI explains client fit; staff owns the recommendation.**
- **Admin is where operational policy is safely changed.**
- **Do not ship a beautiful dashboard over ambiguous states.**

### Action Planning

#### Priority 1: Define MVP State Models

**Why this matters:** Prevents scope creep from producing superficial control without reliable workflows.

**Next steps:**
1. Define allowed statuses and transitions for Report, Gate Finding, Follow-up, and Meeting Brief.
2. Identify which existing data fields support each state and where new fields are needed.
3. Add backend validation so unsafe transitions cannot happen through the UI.

**Success indicator:** Staff cannot approve a report, ready a meeting brief, or complete a follow-up unless the required lifecycle conditions are satisfied.

#### Priority 2: Build Human Review Reliability

**Why this matters:** Safe report delivery is the foundation for client trust and later growth workflows.

**Next steps:**
1. Build Human Review Queue showing escalated reports, severity, owner, age, and next action.
2. Build Gate Findings workspace showing each finding, evidence, reviewer notes, and decision.
3. Block report approval until all findings are resolved, overridden with reason, or escalated further.

**Success indicator:** A report cannot be delivered unless the review lifecycle has reached a valid approved state.

#### Priority 3: Add Follow-ups and Audit Trail

**Why this matters:** Commitments and decisions must not disappear after review or meetings.

**Next steps:**
1. Create follow-up model with owner, due date, source, status, and linked client/report/meeting/opportunity.
2. Record audit events for review decisions, follow-up changes, report approvals, and meeting brief status changes.
3. Surface overdue and blocked items in Command Center and Client Profile.

**Success indicator:** Every operational commitment has an owner, source, status, due date, and visible trail.

#### Priority 4: Ship Simple Meeting Brief

**Why this matters:** Reports should lead into useful client conversations, but meeting automation must stay behind review safety.

**Next steps:**
1. Show upcoming meeting, client/report summary, top recommendations, and prep checklist.
2. Require staff review before marking brief ready.
3. Prevent Ready for Meeting when report review is unresolved or brief is stale.
4. Capture meeting notes and create follow-ups.

**Success indicator:** Staff can prepare for a meeting from approved/current information and convert meeting outcomes into follow-ups.
