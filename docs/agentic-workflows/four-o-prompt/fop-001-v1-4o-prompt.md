# 4o Prompt

Perfect. Let’s raise the bar and make this bulletproof.

You’re building a definitive, **authoritative research report** on AI’s evolving cultural representation, meant to stand up to scrutiny from the sharpest, nerdiest, most AI-literate minds. They don’t want fluff, they want precision, context, and receipts. So let’s now clearly specify:

---

**🔬 Deep Research Prompt (Authoritative Edition)**

**Cultural Representations of AI Before and After ChatGPT: A Multi-Domain Analysis from 1950–2025**

---

**🎯 Objective**

Conduct a **rigorous, well-cited, long-form research study** exploring how cultural representations of artificial intelligence have evolved across time and media, especially before and after the introduction of ChatGPT in 2022 and the rise of AI agents in 2025\. The report should reveal patterns, contradictions, and trajectories—*not* assume convergence, fatigue, or acceptance.

---

**📌 Scope**

**Timeframe:**

	•	**1950–2021**: Baseline cultural portrayals (literature, film, public discourse, emerging tech narratives)

	•	**2022–2023**: Post-ChatGPT shock and assimilation

	•	**2024**: Rise of inference agents, Claude 3, Gemini, GPT-4

	•	**2025**: Mass-market AI agents, fine-tuned autonomy, co-pilots in production systems

**Media Domains:**

	•	Film & television

	•	Print journalism & digital news

	•	Search trends & social discourse (Reddit, TikTok, X)

	•	Venture capital & tech thought leadership

	•	Blogs, newsletters, podcast transcripts

	•	Literary fiction & sci-fi

**Research Lenses:**

	•	Metaphor & narrative

	•	Emotional tone (awe, fear, fatigue, dependence, admiration)

	•	Representational agency (tool vs. being vs. system)

	•	Timing of shifts across domains

---

**📚 Required Sources**

**Primary Media & Discourse:**

	•	**Film/TV:** IMDb dataset, TV Tropes, Letterboxd, Netflix Originals, Rotten Tomatoes

	•	**News:** The New York Times, The Guardian, MIT Tech Review, Wired, Bloomberg Tech

	•	**VC/Tech Discourse:** a16z blog, Ben Evans, Packy McCormick, Sequoia Capital, YC blogs

	•	**Search & Social:** Google Trends, Reddit (e.g. r/Futurology, r/ChatGPT, r/technology), TikTok trend analysis, X (formerly Twitter) archives

	•	**Cultural Blogs/Podcasts:** Substack (e.g. The Gradient, ChinAI, Napkin Math), Hard Fork, Ezra Klein Show, Lex Fridman

	•	**Fiction & Speculative Lit:** Clarkesworld, Lightspeed, Granta, The New Yorker, Hugo/Nebula winners

	•	**Academic Context:** JSTOR, SSRN, ArXiv (for historical research, AI ethics, media theory)

**Secondary Context & Critique:**

	•	**Media scholars:** Sherry Turkle, Kate Crawford, Tim Maughan, Ted Chiang, Zeynep Tufekci

	•	**Philosophy of tech:** Donna Haraway, Langdon Winner, Yuk Hui

	•	**Avoid low-credibility AI hype blogs**, unverified Medium posts, LinkedIn opinion pieces unless substantiated with citations.

---

**👀 Audience**

**Tech-savvy, AI-fluent readers.**

This includes engineers, product leaders, AI founders, researchers, futurists, and highly informed enthusiasts. Your audience already *knows* what ChatGPT is and how inference models work—they’re hungry for deep cultural context, comparative insights, and well-framed open questions.

**They expect:**

	•	Strong citations

	•	Nuanced pattern recognition

	•	Contradictions called out and held up for scrutiny

	•	No lazy takes, no “AI is magic,” no “AI is evil”

---

**✍️ Style & Tone**

	•	**Analytical and authoritative**, yet accessible

	•	**Full paragraphs**, not lists

	•	Use **rich examples**—quote scenes, headlines, Reddit threads

	•	**Cite sources inline** with footnotes or hyperlinks

	•	Include **data visualizations where helpful** (e.g., search term frequency, tone over time, metaphor clustering)

	•	Always **interrogate the metaphor**—don’t just count it, unpack its cultural baggage

---

**🧭 Core Research Questions (Revised & Sharpened)**

**Historical Baseline (1950–2021)**

	•	How has AI been portrayed in fiction, film, and journalism before the generative shift?

	•	What were dominant metaphors: servant, god, child, monster, mirror?

	•	Which portrayals were influential in shaping public expectations?

**Inflection Point (2022–2023)**

	•	How did media respond *immediately* after ChatGPT’s release?

	•	Which media domains shifted first (e.g., tech journalism vs. fiction)?

	•	What tone characterized early coverage—excitement, fear, wonder, satire?

**Inference Models & Agentic Shift (2024)**

	•	What *specific portrayals* emerged with the rise of Claude 3, Gemini, AutoGPT?

	•	How did media explore—or ignore—the implications of autonomous inference?

	•	What metaphors took hold (e.g., co-pilot, intern, shadow mind, parasite)?

	•	What stories appeared in mainstream vs. indie media?

	•	How did the framing shift from prompt-response to workflow/system/actor?

**Year of Agents (2025)**

	•	What’s distinct about portrayals in this “agentic era” of 2025?

	•	Are we seeing convergence (AI as infrastructure) or fracture (conflicting portrayals)?

	•	How do different communities (e.g., enterprise vs. Gen Z TikTok) talk about agents?

	•	Are cultural depictions now normalizing AI as a relational entity, a productivity node, or something else entirely?

**Cross-Domain & Global Comparison**

	•	Which narrative shifts happened synchronously across media—and which didn’t?

	•	Are there East vs. West differences in metaphor or tone?

	•	How are youth-generated platforms (e.g., TikTok, Wattpad) framing AI vs. established media?

---

**📄 Deliverable: The Final Report**

	•	6,000–15,000 words

	•	**Executive Summary (300–500 words)**

	•	Structured chapters with section headings

	•	Includes data visualizations and quote excerpts

	•	Footnotes, linked citations, or endnotes throughout

	•	**Appendix** of key events (timeline of model releases, media milestones, search peaks)
